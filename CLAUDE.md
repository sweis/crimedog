# Crimedog: A Heist Game. For Dogs.

You are a dog criminal mastermind who must assemble a crew of dog criminals to carry out unique heists. You will need to carry out surveillance to plan your heist, buy equipment, 
plan an escape, recruit the right team, fence the goods, and getaway clean. Each dog criminal will have different traits of trustworthiness, loyalty, skills, and talents. 
You will discover these through experience and reputation. Some dogs may be skilled in persuasion, hacking, brute force, physical agility, sharpshooting, disguise, or any number of
up to 100 talents.

As a mastermind, you will need to manage a budget and invest in gathering intelligence, recruiting, equipment, securing safe houses, paying bribes, getting fake identities. You 
won't participate in the heists, but will set the stage and observe how it plays out.

All the criminals and heist heist is randomly generated to give unique plays every time. Some heists may succeed and yield money and reputation. Successful heists will make it easier
to recruit criminals and build relationships with the successful crew. Failed heists will do the opposite. There are many ways to fail including tripping alarms and being arrested,
being betrayed out by disloyal crew, being overpowered by guards, or leaving too many clues behind for investigators. Individual crew members may get captured and interrogated. Based
on their traits, they may reveal the plan or not. That doesn't guarantee the heist fails, but reveals information to investigators and gives them an advantage in catching the rest of
the crew.

Crew are also not guaranteed to succeed in a planned step and may fail. If that occurs, the plan will go "Pear Shaped" and crew members will improvise and try to succeed. There will be
an element of chaos that is unpredictable how it turns out and heists may end up being partial successes, e.g. some of the loot is recovered or some of the crew is arrested. Crew
members who stay loyal will be sent to the pound to do their time. The mastermind will be able to help them by sending money to reduce their sentences. 

Solving each heist is open ended. The mastermind will need to pick different crew members to attempt different stages and make use of different equipment. Each stage may need several
prerequisites like having a crew member placed in a specific location or using a specific piece of equipment. Heists can be very different in nature. Some may be a brute force smash
and grab where the crew simply overpowers guards and tries to get away. Some may be intricate with multiple interlocking steps. There should be multiple paths to succeed and it should
support adaptability and improvisation.

The nemesis in the game is The Inspector who will try to collect clues that lead back to the mastermind. As they get more information, they will start setting up stings with fake
fences and undercover crew members. The mastermind will rely on crew's experience and reputation and what they learn over time to know who to trust. They will also be able to surveil
criminals and try to find undercover agents. 

At the end of heists, the mastermind will also have the option to send crew members to "live on a farm" and be taken out of the game. This is a betrayal and impacts the mastermind's
reputation and likelihood of being betrayed themselves.

Completed heists will be graded. The perfect heist is not detected, the loot will be fenced at full value, the crew paid out, and everyone escapes according to plan. The victim may not
even discover the heist happened. The game ending heist is if the mastermind is discovered by the inspector, runs out of all resources, or has a reputation that drops too low that 
nobody will work for them.

Gameplay should be turn based in phases. The mastermind can recruit talent, then depending on what skills they have, deploy them for different tasks. Smart play would require them to 
do several rounds of intelligence gathering, buying tools and resources, and planning. However, it should allow them to be reckless and go into a heist with no planning and high 
likelihood of failure. For example, they can hire an unknown criminal and send them to rob something with no plan. This might actually be a strategy for intelligence gathering or as a
distraction.

Playthrough of a single heist should take 5-10 minutes depending on how much time and complexity the mastermind opts for. Each heist should be uniquely generated and could have a
memorable name or mnemonic, e.g. "The Case of the Golden Bone" or "The Kibble Job". 

Graphically, it can be stylized and does not need to be 3d or complex. It should be easy to render and play on a phone, so the interface should be simple and playable with a
touchscreen. It's meant to be light and fun, but be challenging to pull off a very good heist. Kids should enjoy it, but it can still be a puzzle for adults.

Stylistically, nobody in the game should acknowledge being a dog, but there should be dog-themed jokes and references. The loot should be a mix of human-themed loot (cash, jewelry, 
art) and dog-themed (squeaky toy, a rare bone). The setting should be in a London-like setting and inspired by Guy Ritchie movies like Lock Stock and Two Smoking Barrels, Snatch, The
Gentlemen. This should be seen in interacting with dogs with Posh or Cockney accents. Different dog breeds might be their own groups, like German Shepherds reprsenting Germans (and
referred to as "Ze Germans" in a nod to Snatch). Don't actually refer to any real world places, nationalities, or ethnic groups. Just refer to dog breeds.

Classic heist movies like Oceans 11, Heat, and Reservoir Dogs are all inspirations. Including references to those movies or using character archetypes is good. Part of the
goal of the game is to over time find and discover criminals and learn more about them until they are an eclectic, trusted crew. Part of the fun of the game will be to have that
collection of characters and figure out how to use them successfully. Add a way to share a snapshot of a character with details about them like a name, specialty, catchphrase, and
relationship.
